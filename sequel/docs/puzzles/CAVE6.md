# Cave 6, redesigned: how the lock works, and how it is played (draft for the user's approval, 2026-10-30)

The user's layout (a drawn section, then): one small opening in the ceiling, straight above an opening in the floor; a
lower chamber under it, down a rope ladder; seven discs in a row in floor sockets at the bottom; the triangles on the
upper cave's back wall (three small, the great one in the middle, three small); Canario wall paintings in the lower
chamber "so Vasco will have something to read". And: "the beam of light from the top should always cover all of the
disks, it's the disks that focus it, reflecting it"; "the central disk re-directs the light to the central big
triangle"; "the finale should be about the order of the disks, not the angle"; "which all means that the locking needs to happen
exactly at noon".

## The lock (lore)

- **Noon.** The small opening is the bottom of a shaft cut straight down through the rock from the top of the cliff.
  Only when the sun stands at its highest does its light reach the bottom: through the summer months, a broad column of
  sunlight comes down the shaft at noon, through the floor opening, and falls on the whole row of seven sockets at once.
  At the summer turning the sun at Risco Caído stands almost straight overhead (85° at 28° N): the column is at its
  fullest. The lock is renewed, and can be opened or shut, **at noon on the day the sun turns back: 24 June.** True noon,
  by the sun: **14:05 Canary summer time** (UTC+1, in force in 1996; Risco Caído at 15.66° W puts the sun's noon an hour
  after the clock's, and the equation of time adds two minutes that week), the sun 85.4° up. The user: "the canary summer
  time please - this is important to know". Vasco knows it to the minute.
- Each disc is a shallow obsidian mirror, ground to its own curve, with a sign cut in its back: seven Canario signs, the
  patterns of the island's own clay seals (pintaderas): a spiral, a zigzag (the sea), concentric rings, a chequer, a
  triangle, the half-circle fan ("the crescent" in these notes: the moon half gone into the sea; a crescent as such is
  not a Canario sign), and the rayed sun, Magec. The same seven signs are cut, worn almost smooth, at the point of the
  seven triangles above, and round each triangle a scene (see "The order").
- In its own socket, a disc gathers the light and throws a column of it back up through the floor opening onto its own
  triangle. The sun disc, in the middle, lights the great triangle. Every morning, seven triangles lit: the sentence read
  out, the lock kept true.
- A disc taken out: its triangle stays dark; the sentence has a hole in it. That is why the lock loosened a nail at a
  time, from December to June, and why the mist and the tides got worse disc by disc.
- **The door order.** Set the discs in one other order and every column, instead of rising to a triangle, falls on one
  point: the centre of the lower chamber's floor, the door. At noon on a turning day that opens it. Elena found that order
  (her pencil line, "Puerta"); Vasco works it out on Saturday and refuses it to Baute; he swallows the corner of the
  chart with it on.
- **To close it:** at noon on the turning day, every disc back in its own socket, set by someone standing in the Sea's water,
  which comes up through the door into the lower chamber. When the seventh triangle lights, the door shuts and the
  water goes down through it, and takes whoever is standing in it.
- **Baute's plan, Monday:** he has only the sun disc and the crescent, the last he took (in May), which he keeps on him. At four in the morning he went down
  and moved the sun disc from the middle into the socket at the end of the row, the one that, alone, throws Magec's
  column straight onto the door. With six discs gone, the door doesn't need the whole order: "close will do".

## Saturday: Vasco's chapter (replaces the chalk marks, the disc turned to steer, the clinometer)

1. **The camp** (as now): Yeray's coffee and "not going to Las Palmas"; Elena's charts on the finds table. Her charts
   are now drawings of the row of sockets with a sign by each, crossed out and redrawn, and on the last sheet a second
   arrangement in pencil, with every line converging on the floor, and in the margin, underlined three times:
   *Puerta.*
2. **The cave, at noon** (he waits for it; Saturday is a turning day too, the light is near its fullest): the column of light down through the opening; only one triangle lit (the great one). Looking at the dark triangles: "Something cut at the point. Worn smooth."
   - **Chalk on a triangle** (an archaeologist's trick: chalk dust rubbed into a worn cut makes it read): each triangle's
     sign, one at a time; his notebook fills in a row of seven signs, as they stand on the wall.
   - **The scenes round the triangles:** he reads the lit one (Magec) and, by his torch, the one beside it (the moon going down) and knows them: "It's
     the grandmothers' romance. The women and the moons. The order is in a song, and I don't have the words." He reads
     the others into his notebook; the radio cuts him off.
3. **Down the rope ladder, the lower chamber:** six empty sockets and one disc, the sun in the middle (the discs came out
   one a month since December; the sixth, the crescent, in May). The column of light on all seven sockets; one column
   rising, to the great triangle.
   - **The frieze**, read panel by panel (the women in white on the cliff; the ships; the black sea over the ships and
     the houses; the women on the shore beating the sea with branches; Magec; the cave with its seven circles, each with
     its sign, and a line rising from each; the woman standing in the water). The cave panel is the key: the order of
     the signs along the floor.
   - **The test:** he lifts the sun disc and sets it in the next socket along. Its column jumps to a small triangle
     on the other side (the columns cross), and the great triangle goes dark; the water stirs in the door below. "They're keyed. Each disc has its word, and its place
     in the sentence." He puts it back.
   - **Elena's charts on the frieze:** her pencil arrangement is the frieze's order turned round: every column on the
     floor. The door order. "It isn't counting days... It's holding something down." He has both orders now: the one
     that reads the sentence and the one that opens the door. He tears off the corner with the door order on it, and
     swallows it.
4. **The radio, Baute, the vault** (as now). In the vault the mark he scratches by the grille is **the seven signs in
   their order, and an A** (not 23° 26′).

## Monday: the finale (an order puzzle; the player's, not Vasco's calls)

- **Morning, the cave.** They come along the cliff path at dawn; Baute on his stool. Noon is the hour; the morning is
  the clock, the water rising a hand a half-hour up the rope ladder from the door. A few minutes before noon: The column of light down through the opening; Magec's column rising
  not to the great triangle but straight back down onto the floor of the lower chamber: the sun disc moved to the end of
  the row. Water coming up through the floor opening. Vasco looks down at it the way he looks at an altarpiece: "He's
  moved the sun. It's on the door."
- **Alan goes down the rope ladder** ("It's coming for me anyway"), into the water rising round his legs. He has five
  discs from the vault, each now its own thing in his pockets: the spiral disc, the zigzag disc, the rings disc, the
  chequer disc, the triangle disc. Vasco kneels at the rim of the floor opening, above, reading the triangles.
- **Stuck.** Alan has the five discs and where they go (Vasco's vault mark), but not when. Vasco, above, reads him the
  scenes; without the telling there's no order. One disc set by guess is a surge.
- **Baute's last help.** Through the cave mouth Baute sees the black stain spreading over Las Palmas; Carmen names her
  street (as now). He breaks: he says his grandmother's telling, verse by verse, from the stool, in Spanish (Vasco
  echoes each verse down in English), and gives up the disc he kept on him: the crescent, May's.
- **The puzzle:** each verse matched to its scene (a notebook page, "The grandmothers' telling": seven verses, a sign
  to choose for each from the scenes Vasco has read); then each disc into its socket, in the telling's order, and
  aimed at its triangle. Alan sets zigzag, chequer, triangle, rings, spiral, then the crescent.
- **The sun disc:** last, Alan has to lift Magec out of the door socket and set it in the middle. As he reaches for it,
  a cloud crosses the sun (as now): no light, no columns, nothing to tell right from wrong, and the water at his chest.
  Carmen tears the wing mirror off the Land Rover, runs up the path to the top of the rock where the shaft comes out,
  and throws the sunlight down it by hand ("Venga, you ugly thing"). In her light, Alan sets the sun disc in the middle: the great triangle
  lights, and every triangle with it, all seven at once. The cloud slides off, and the real sun comes.
- **The lock** (as now): the mountain breathes out, the water goes down through the door and takes Alan with it; the
  chain of hands: Vasco reaching down through the floor opening to his wrist, Carmen holding Vasco, Yeray Carmen, Baute
  Yeray. They haul him up through the opening.

## The order: where each disc goes, and when (the riddle)

The user: "it matters which disks goes in place in which order. It shouldn't just be left to right - otherwise there is
no riddle. We also need a reason for Vasco to be upstairs - so I'd put additional inscriptions around the triangles too,
so he can read them and give the directions to Alan who is downstairs."

Two questions, two halves of the cave: **where** each disc goes (its socket) and **when** it goes in (its turn). The
lock is a sentence said in light. Said in the wrong order, it is another sentence, and the Sea hears it.

### Where: the signs at the triangles' points, read in a mirror
- Each triangle has its disc's sign cut at its point. The columns cross in the floor opening, so the floor is the wall
  reversed: socket 1 (far left) lights triangle 7 (far right), and so on. The sun is in the middle both ways.
- The wall, left to right: rings, spiral, zigzag, **sun**, crescent, triangle, chequer.
- So the floor, left to right: chequer, triangle, crescent, **sun**, zigzag, spiral, rings.
- Vasco works this out on Saturday (the chalk; the sun-disc test) and scratches it in the vault. Alan has it in his
  notebook on Monday.

### When: the grandmothers' telling (Baute's)
The user: "the order ... will be coming from an old canarian legend - that vasco knows that exists and figures out it's
the key, but only Baute knows precisely from his grandmother - this will be Baute's final help ... and it will also
explain why he removed the disks one by one, each month starting in December".

**The telling.** The Artenara grandmothers keep a telling (a *romance* said at the hearth, never written) of how the
women put the Sea back to bed: not in one night but over half a year, one stone a moon, from the winter turning to the
summer turning. Each verse is a moon and a stone. Said in order, it locks; said backwards, word by word at the same
moons, it unlocks. That is why Baute, who had it from his grandmother in Tasarte, took the discs out **one a month,
from the night of 20 December**: he was unsaying it at the moons the women said it, in their order. Nobody else knew
which disc went when: Yeray carried them down in a sack, and the transfer sheets only say "disco de obsidiana".

| Moon (Baute took it out) | Verse (Baute's grandmother, as he says it) | Sign | Scene round its triangle |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1, the winter turning (20 December) | "When the sun was smallest, the water stood up taller than the mountain, and the wind could not push it." | zigzag | the sea standing up black, the trees bent the other way |
| 2 (January) | "In the second moon it lay down in our houses and slept there." | chequer | fields and houses under water, a boat on a roof |
| 3 (February) | "In the third moon the white ones came down the goat paths." | triangle | the women in white coming down the cliff paths |
| 4 (March) | "In the fourth moon they gave it the goats' milk, and it drank." | rings | the women pouring jars into the sea, rings spreading |
| 5 (April) | "In the fifth moon they beat it like a dog, and it went down to its bed." | spiral | the women beating the sea with branches, the water turning down |
| 6 (May) | "In the sixth moon one stayed in the water, and the moon went in with her." | crescent | the moon going down into the sea, one woman standing in it |
| 7, the summer turning (noon, 24 June) | "And on the long day, at the top of the day, they set the sun in the middle, and the year began." | sun | Magec |

The verses don't name the signs: each has to be matched to the scene round its triangle, which only Vasco, above, can
see. That matching is the player's riddle on Monday.

**Real roots.** Abreu Galindo writes that the Canarios counted the year by moons and began it at the summer turning,
with days of feasting (to check against the text before any line uses it); the harimaguadas; his drought rite (the
women carrying milk and palm branches down to the sea and beating it with sticks). The telling itself, and the lock, are
the game's.

**Who knows what.**
- **Vasco** knows the telling exists. He heard of it in Artenara and finds it in the scenes round the triangles on
  Saturday ("It's the grandmothers' romance. The women and the moons. That's the key: the order is in a song, and I
  don't have the words."). He can read every scene; he can't put them in order. Six moons is too many orders to guess
  with the water rising.
- **Baute** has the words, from his grandmother. On Saturday, in the vault, Vasco works out why the discs went one a
  month ("You were saying it backwards. Who taught you?") and Baute says nothing.
- **Carmen** knows the first line, as a lullaby, and no more (a nice touch on Sunday; optional).

### How it's played on Monday
- Alan, downstairs in the rising water, with five discs; Vasco, upstairs at the rim of the floor opening with his torch,
  reading the wall. Alan can't see the triangles' scenes; Vasco can't reach the discs. That's why Vasco stays up.
- Talk to Vasco: "Read me the one with the [sign]". He describes the scene and what's at its edge. Alan's notebook
  gets a page, "The story on the wall", seven blanks to fill with signs (the existing pages mechanic, `S.PAGES`).
- **Aim it.** The user: "after each positioning of the disk, it has to be set to an angle that would point at the
  correct triangle - so the last disk lights up the first triangle etc. ... watching the beam move in real time". A disc
  sits on a rounded base in its socket and rocks. Once it's in, the player tilts it (mouse left and right, or the arrow
  keys): its column sweeps across the wall above, seen through the floor opening, in real time. The columns cross in the
  opening, so a disc on the right reaches the triangles on the left. Click (or Enter) to set it where it is.
- **Three ways to be wrong**, each a surge (the water a hand higher; three and the Sea takes him: death screen, retry
  from the checkpoint): the column set on another disc's triangle (Vasco: "That's the rings' triangle!"); a disc set out
  of its turn in the story; a disc in the wrong socket can't reach its own triangle at all. Set on bare rock: nothing
  happens, aim again.
- **Right**: the column holds, the patch of light settles on the triangle, and Vasco reads that scene aloud as it lights.
- **When the seventh is set**: the triangles flare up with light; the harimaguadas, made of light, outlines only, come
  out of the columns and dance in a ring round the noon column, arms raised with their branches; then the women and the
  triangles' glow fade, and it's only the columns of light again. (The user's idea; the one time the cave shows what it
  is. Not the Sea: the women.) Then the door shuts and takes Alan (as before).
- Prototype: `art/review/cave6_redesign/aim.html` (`aim_play.html` is the same, with the painting inside the file),
  over the lower-chamber painting; the light follows `beams_render.py`. The women are drawn by code there, as
  placeholders: in the game they'd be painted (or animated frames), still only outlines of light.
- The first five are the player's. Then Baute and the crescent (as before), then the sun out of the door socket and into
  the middle, under Carmen's mirror.

## What this changes in the bible
- The discs came out one a month, from 20 December to May (not disc 1 in December and 2–5 that week): the mist and the
  tides got worse moon by moon, which fits "the mist and the tides got worse disc by disc". The lines that date the
  removals (the transfer sheets, Pino's list of the dates, Carmen's "disc one came out in December") are checked and
  any that clash are rewritten.
- Baute's grandmother's telling joins his backstory (Tasarte, 1966): it's what he inherited instead of a grave.

## The noon, through the story

Everything that ties the lock to the sunrise of the 24th moves to its noon (about 25 lines; the list in the work):
Vasco's "the sunrise on the twenty-fourth is the whole point" and Friday's "the sunrise on Monday"; Carmen's "the
morning el sol turns back" (the day); Baute's map, "24·VI · mediodía"; the 1484 inquiry and the bible (the sea went back
at noon on Saint John's day; the bishop's miracle); the letter ("at the noon of the turning"); Sunday on the rock ("What
time is noon, cariño?" "Five past two. By your watch."); Monday's clock and the Guardia (in the afternoon);
the stills "sunrise" and "the chain" (painted again in noon light). The San Juan night itself (the bonfires, the city in
the sea at midnight) stays as it is: the shortest night, then the noon of the turn.
