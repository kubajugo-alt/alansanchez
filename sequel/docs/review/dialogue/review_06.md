# Dialogue review: Saturday (Vasco) and the mountain

Overall: sense 4/5, naturalism 4/5. Vasco's voice is the chapter's great strength: the lecturer coming alive over the chalk and the light, the dry jokes about Rayco's "original fabric" and the cat flap, the quiet one-word answers to Baute. The refusal and the prayer land with real weight. The problems are mostly about what Vasco knows: three times he talks as if he already knows things he only learns later or never learns (the vault, the word "under", Rayco keeping the sixth disc on him), and twice his lines ignore what he does know (he signed the transfer sheets, so empty sockets can't surprise him; he has been on the dig for weeks without opening Elena's charts). There are smaller slips too: five hundred years against nine hundred, and the discs in straw against discs in cloth.

## S.startVasco
Sense 5/5 · Naturalism 4/5. A good opener: the narrator sets up the joke and Vasco's denial lands it. Nothing to flag.

## room camp
Sense 4/5 · Naturalism 4/5. Good, sparse examine lines; Yeray "carrying something heavy all night" is a nice plant. The weak spot is the charts: Vasco has worked a month in Elena's place, yet her papers are "filed and forgotten" and he only opens them today, with no reason given.
- `vasco_look_finds` (Vasco) **should**: "The expedition files, in grey boxes. At the back of the last one, my predecessor's papers, filed and forgotten." → "The expedition files, in grey boxes. At the back of the last one, Elena's papers. Rayco said they were nothing. I believed him." Without a reason, a meticulous scholar who never read his predecessor's charts in a month is not believable; this gives him one, and it points at Baute.

## S.yerayCoffee
Sense 4/5 · Naturalism 5/5. The best small scene in the chapter: Yeray's half-finished sentences, "Then yes.", "It's the national sport." The trouble is that Yeray drops two bombs and Vasco's exit line only picks up one.
- `vasco_v_yeray_4` (Vasco) **should**: "A Portuguese guy at Rafa's bar. Funny. Sad underneath. [pause] I'm not thinking about Alan." → "Not going to Las Palmas. [pause] A Portuguese guy at Rafa's bar. Sad underneath. [pause] I'm not thinking about Alan." Rule 2: "They're not going to Las Palmas" is the chapter's first plot clue, and Vasco walks off without even turning it over once.

## S.takeCharts
Sense 4/5 · Naturalism 4/5. The "Puerta" reveal is well paced. Small repetition only.
- `vasco_take_1` (Vasco) **could**: "Elena Suárez. My predecessor. Rayco said her name once, the first day, flat as a wall: an accident in December, on the mountain road." → "Elena Suárez. Rayco said her name once, the first day, flat as a wall: an accident in December, on the mountain road." "My predecessor" has already been said at the finds table a moment ago (or there is now "Elena's papers", if the fix above is taken).

## S.lookRadio
Sense 4/5 · Naturalism 4/5. "And say what?" and "I don't want to talk to him either" are lovely. The list in `vasco_v_see_0` pulls the clues together well.
- `vasco_v_see` (Vasco) **could**: "I haven't wanted to see this. I've walked past it every day for a week, because I didn't want to see it." → "I've walked past it every day for a week. [pause] Because I didn't want to see it." The line says "didn't want to see" twice in one breath; the bible's own quote is just the second half.

## S.foilRadio
Sense 3/5 · Naturalism 4/5. The foil is a good, physical beat, and Baute's arrival is chilling. But Vasco's self-reproach claims a knowledge he hasn't got: he has no idea yet where the discs go (he learns the granary only when he wakes in it), so "the only word that mattered" doesn't follow. Vasco also lets Baute's smooth lie about the radio pass without a word; that is acceptable only because the refusal follows at once.
- `vasco_r_after` (Vasco) **should**: "[pause] 'Under.' Well done, Vasco. A whole sentence about where, and you stopped at the only word that mattered." → "[pause] 'Under.' Under what, Vasco? You don't even know. [pause] And now nobody will." `vasco_r_send` has to stay (Lolo hears it and Alan quotes it), but Vasco is guessing "down, under" from Yeray and the arrows, and his reaction should admit that.
- `vasco_r_foil_1` (Vasco) **could**: "Folded twice. Into the holder. [pause] God forgive me, Lorenzo, I'm doing electrics." → "Folded twice. Into the holder. [pause] Forgive me, Lorenzo. I'm doing electrics." Calling on God and Lorenzo in one breath muddles the joke (who is he apologising to?); Lorenzo the architect is the joke.

## room cave6
Sense 4/5 · Naturalism 4/5. Vasco's looks are a historian's, as they should be ("Fertility symbols, the textbooks say... They're arrows"). Alan's Monday lines are fine, though "At the door" closes three of his looks in a row (`alan_look_great`, `alan_look_triangles`, `alan_look_door`); that is just about tolerable as a refrain.
- `vasco_ladder_dark` (Vasco) **should**: "Down there, with a torch that's dying? Not yet. Let the sun go down first. It does, every noon." → "Down there, with a torch that's dying? Not yet. Let the sun go first. It climbs down every noon." "Let the sun go down" means sunset to any listener, and that contradicts the noon puzzle he is pointing the player at.
- `alan_look_yeray_cave` (Alan) **could**: "Yeray, at the back, very pale. He'll be all right. He doesn't know that yet." → "Yeray, at the back, very pale. Somebody should tell him he'll be all right. Not now." Alan can't know how things turn out at that moment; as written it is narrator foresight in Alan's mouth.

## room cave6_low
Sense 5/5 · Naturalism 4/5. "No. Not wrong. Turned round." is a fine beat; Alan's "It's patient. It's very, very patient" is the right kind of not-joke near the water. Nothing to flag.

## S.lookSocket
Sense 3/5 · Naturalism 4/5. "I look like a man who hasn't slept" is good. But Vasco countersigned the conservation transfer sheets, so he knows the six discs are out; "Someone's been prying these out" plays as a discovery he can't be making. The real clue is how they came out.
- `vasco_look_sockets` (Vasco) **should**: "Fresh tool marks round the empty ones. Steel, not stone. Someone's been prying these out with a modern chisel." → "Fresh tool marks round the empty ones. Steel, not stone. A chisel, in a hurry. [pause] No conservator lifts a disc like that." This keeps the purpose (the discs were not taken out for conservation) and fits what he signed. The same line also plays in S.useSocket.

## S.useSocket
Sense 4/5 · Naturalism 4/5. The same line as in S.lookSocket; the fix above covers it.

## S.chalkSigns
Sense 5/5 · Naturalism 4/5. "They lit the moons with the sun. That's the leash." is Vasco at his best: the lecturer, talking with his hands about light. Nothing to flag.

## S.readScenes
Sense 4/5 · Naturalism 4/5. A clear setup for Monday's telling.
- `vasco_scenes_2` (Vasco) **could**: "It's the grandmothers' romance. The women and the moons." → "It's the grandmothers' telling. The women and the moons." "Romance" is the Spanish term for a ballad, but an English player hears a love story; "telling" is the word Baute and the finale use, so the two days would match.

## S.waitNoon
Sense 5/5 · Naturalism 4/5. Clean and correct (sun two hours behind the clocks, noon at 14:04). Nothing to flag.

## S.sunTest
Sense 5/5 · Naturalism 5/5. "I felt something listen" and "Back. Quickly." are exactly how the Sea should be handled: felt, never shown. Nothing to flag.

## S.understand
Sense 4/5 · Naturalism 3/5. The logic is sound and the swallow is a strong beat. But the scene piles up metaphors: a leash (from the chalk), a sentence and its words, a door, nails. Each is good alone, but together they turn the understanding into a lecture. It stays acceptable because Vasco is a lecturer and this is his peak, and the turn to "Alan." and "I said, go home." rescues it. No single line is broken enough to re-record.

## S.refusal
Sense 4/5 · Naturalism 4/5. Baute is warm, precise and unhurried, and the Tasarte story earns its length; "It's very inconvenient" is perfect. Three continuity problems remain: one choice names a vault Vasco hasn't seen, one asks where a woman he knows is dead "is", and Baute's dates contradict the cave's.
- `vasco_v_07b` (Vasco) **must**: "Rayco. What's in the vault?" → "Rayco. Where are the discs?" Vasco knows nothing about a vault until he wakes in it (Baute even introduces it: "Mira. A Canario granary"); all he has is Yeray's "They're not going to Las Palmas", and "Rayco doesn't deny it" still works after the new line.
- `vasco_v_09b` (Vasco) **should**: "Where's Dr. Suárez, Rayco?" → "What happened to Dr. Suárez, Rayco?" Vasco knows she died in December, so "where's" makes no sense; the point of the question is the cause, and Baute's answer is about the cause.
- `baute_v_03` (Baute) **should**: "Every summer for five hundred years el sol has come through that hole..." → "Every summer for nine hundred years el sol has come through that hole..." The same chapter dates the cave to nine hundred years (`vasco_look_rock`, `vasco_look_walls`, `alan_socket_empty`, the granary "twelfth century"), and Baute, an archaeologist, would not get his own site's date wrong by four centuries.

## S.toVault
Sense 4/5 · Naturalism 5/5. The drug sequence is excellent ("Before your legs do it for you"; "like an earring on a corpse"; "Elena kept it in the camp for her back"). One line doesn't follow.
- `vasco_v8_keys` (Vasco) **should**: "The keys aren't in the Land Rover. Or on the hook. [pause] You've been down to the village. [pause] Then I'll walk." → "The keys aren't in the Land Rover. Or on the hook. [pause] You've had them all morning. [pause] Then I'll walk." Going down to the village doesn't explain missing keys (he would have come back with the car and the keys); the line should say plainly that Rayco has taken them.
- `vasco_v_12` (Vasco) **could**: "Your word's under a hotel, Rayco." → "Elena had your word too, Rayco." The current line sneers at the Tasarte dead, which a man who loves the dead as Vasco does would not do; the rewrite strikes at the real wound.

## room vault
Sense 4/5 · Naturalism 4/5. Vasco's looks are superb ("Rayco always did respect the original fabric"; the cat flap; "Like a dog"), and Alan's Sunday lines have the right relief ("Three idiots and a pick"). But the crates contradict themselves and each other, and Vasco knows something he can't.
- `vasco_look_crates_2` (Vasco) **should**: "Five here. The sun's still in the cave, and Rayco keeps the sixth on him. On Monday he'll tip the sun by guesswork. [pause] And he thinks he can't do it without me." → "Five here. Six sockets empty, the sun still in the cave. [pause] So Rayco has the sixth. On Monday he'll tip the sun by guesswork. And he thinks he can't do it without me." Nobody has told Vasco that Baute carries the sixth disc; he has to work it out, which also suits his voice better. (The line also plays in vault_cell.)
- `vasco_look_crates` (Vasco) **could**: "...An obsidian disc in each, in straw. The missing nails. All here." → "...An obsidian disc in each, in straw. The missing nails. [pause] Five of them." "All here" is contradicted by the next look ("Five here"), and there are six empty sockets. (The line also plays in vault_cell.)
- `alan_look_crates` (Alan) **could**: "Five crates. In each, a black glass disc wrapped in cloth." → "Five crates. In each, a black glass disc in straw." Vasco's look at the same crates says straw; the two should match.

## room vault_cell
Sense 4/5 · Naturalism 4/5. The same lines as the vault's Saturday ones (the fixes above apply), plus "It might as well be Lisbon", which is right. Nothing new to flag.

## S.scratchMark
Sense 5/5 · Naturalism 5/5. The heart of the chapter: the trowel, "I thought I had", the A, and "Ah. So it's me." echoing Pepe. The joke about his mother's letters of complaint is in voice. Nothing to flag.

## S.dig
Sense 5/5 · Naturalism 4/5. Short, physical and well paced; the snapped blade ("Of course.") is the right size. Nothing to flag.

## S.vaultEnd
Sense 4/5 · Naturalism 4/5. The prayer works: the specific images of Alan, the admission, and "send the idiot". Its one weak line is the apology at its start.
- `vasco_v_14` (Vasco) **should**: "I'm sorry. I've been working on a Saturday. [pause] I know You're busy." → "I'm sorry. It's been since January. [pause] I know You're busy." A Catholic doesn't apologise to God for working on a Saturday, and the excuse doesn't connect to six months of silence; the narrator has just said "for the first time since January", so Vasco should own it.
