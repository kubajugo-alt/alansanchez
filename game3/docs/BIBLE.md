# Alan Sanches III: The Forgotten Rhythm — Game Bible

Nov 3, 2026 · @Kuba

## Overview

*The Forgotten Rhythm* is a point-and-click adventure in Santo Domingo, 4–11 September 1996, as Hurricane Hortense approaches. Alan and Inês are both playable: Alan works the streets and the people, Inês works the archive and the institutions. Together they must put the founders' bell back in its tower and raise a shore big enough to turn the Sea.

**Premise.** The auxiliary bishop is about to melt the cathedral's 1538 Lisbon bell, one of the founders' "true ones". The bell has been owed to the Sea since Drake's sack in 1586. For 410 years one family of women, the Brans, paid the debt every hurricane season with drums and songs on the shore below the fortress. Doña Tata, the last of them, drowned in her bed in April. Nobody has paid this year, and the bishop is taking the bell out.

**Series position.** This is game 3 of the Atlantic route (Lisbon → Las Palmas → Santo Domingo → St. Augustine). The theme is community and friendship, with the Alan–Inês friendship at its centre. Alan and Vasco are secure as a couple and "play together". Inês is now Vasco's assistant and travels with them in person for the first time, which leaves Alan feeling surplus.

### Design pillars

1.  **Two halves make one song.** Every system mirrors the novel's core image: Tata's Spanish *salves* plus Yolande's Kreyòl answer, Alan's people plus Inês's papers, Dominican plus Haitian.

2.  **Listening is a verb.** Alan's skill is getting people to talk. Recruitment and dialogue puzzles reward noticing, remembering and bringing the right thing, never charm stats.

3.  **The Sea plays fair.** Every rule of the Sea is shown on screen before the finale needs it.

4.  **The finale is played, not watched.** Each beat the novel gives to someone else is something the player earned earlier.

5.  **The novel is the tone reference.** Scenes breathe; the best lines survive word for word.

## Rules of the Sea

Every rule below is stated on screen before the finale depends on it. The finale must not introduce a new rule.

**Series canon.** The Sea is older than the gods. It is woken by the wrong sound at the wrong time and feeds on fear. In game 1 it let Alan go because he is the blood of the bell-makers. Game 2 belonged to the sun; game 3's cycle is the hurricane season.

### Signs that it is near

Salt water where none should be; a cold smell "like a well that has not been opened"; the bell sweating; birds leaving the river mouth; a dark patch beyond the reef that moves against the swell; a tide that comes in and will not go out. Alan's heartbeat (three beats and a rest, a skip in the third) grows louder as it nears (see Core mechanics).

## Core mechanics

Standard point-and-click verbs (look, talk, use, give, combine) plus ten systems built for this game.

### 1. Two playable halves

- **Alan** plays streets and people: barrios, market, bar, gallera, recruits. His puzzles are dialogue, observation and bringing the right thing.

- **Inês** plays papers and institutions: archive, dinner, fax, foundry. Her puzzles are palaeography, cross-referencing, document assembly and pressure dialogue.

- Control switches at fixed points, shown as a page-turn with the napkin. The player never chooses who to play; the story does.

- They share one inventory of *documents* (copies, faxes, notebook pages) but keep separate *object* inventories. A document one finds becomes usable by the other after the next reunion scene.

- **Vasco** is a companion character for both. He comments, translates church Latin and Spanish subjunctives, and is the drum in the finale. He is never playable.

### 2. The napkin (quest log)

Inês writes it at Sunday breakfast; it exists from then on as the game's log. It has two columns split by a fold.

- **1. BELL** (Inês): Paris dossier, Peralta, Rome, the foundry, the tower.

- **2. SHORE** (Alan): every recruit, written in two sub-columns, *Salves* (Dominican) and *Kreyòl* (Haitian).

Before Sunday, a smaller version lives in Alan's notebook as "Things I know". The finale checks that both SHORE sub-columns hold their required names, not a headcount. The crowd number (16 → 123) is narrative and is shown only as people arriving.

### 3. Recruitment

Each recruit is a small chain with three beats: find them, learn what stopped them going to the shore, and give them a reason drawn from their own story. Wrong offers get a polite no and a hint, never a dead end. Full chains are in the day sections; the summary is in the dependency map.

### 4. The heartbeat (Sea proximity)

A small pulse icon over Alan's portrait beats in the series rhythm: three beats and a rest, with a skip in the third. It is dormant until the tower on Wednesday, then grows louder and faster as the Sea nears. It is the game's only meter.

- It tells the player a Sea event is close and is the cue for **Sea grip** sequences.

- From Sunday (after Lilo), Alan can *tap the rhythm* on drums and his own chest. This is the only rhythm input in the game: tap three, rest, with the skip. It has a generous timing window. One global "relaxed timing" setting covers every timed section in the game (the tide chase, the vèvè study, the raid choice, the fax, the foundry clock, the winch gusts and the rhythm): it doubles every window and lets the rhythm auto-play.

- Alan's look darkens through the week as in game 2: paler, salt at the hairline, wet cuffs.

### 5. Sea grip

Short sequences where Alan cannot move and the water rises: the market drain (Sat) and the gallera drum (Sun) are taught moments, and the finale's phase 4 is the big one. During a grip the screen narrows, sound drops to drums and water, and the cursor offers only things *other people* can do for him. The lesson is the game's theme: he survives because someone else acts. If the player misses the cue, the Sea takes Alan: a short drowning cutscene, then a rewind to the moment before (see Deaths and failure). In the finale a missed beat replays with a stronger hint instead.

### 6. The parrot (hint system)

Señora Mireya's parrot repeats overheard key lines when clicked: "¡No se permiten!", "¡Huracán!", and one line from the latest unsolved chain each day ("¡Dos botellas!", "¡La señora del Gulbenkian!"). Three clicks give a direct hint in Mireya's voice from the kitchen. The parrot is a global icon in the corner of every screen, for Inês as well as Alan (in the archive it mutters what Padre Ulises would say). On Monday the parrot goes to Mireya's neighbour; the icon becomes Tobías's ring of keys for Act III, still global.

### 7. The daily clocks

Two visible clocks tick every day, as the tide steps and the bonfires did in game 2.

- **The hurricane chart.** Mireya keeps the *Listín Diario* tracking map pinned on the kitchen wall. Each morning the player reads the radio bulletin's latitude and longitude and places that day's pin. The track bends toward the island; from Sunday the forecast cone covers Santo Domingo. A wrong pin gets a tut, and Mireya moves it.

- **The patch.** From the roof each night the dark patch sits closer: two miles out on Wednesday, the reef on Friday, inside the swell on Tuesday.

### 8. The breakfast inspection (daily comic puzzle)

Three mornings (Thursday, Sunday and Monday), at 07:00, Alan gets three actions before Mireya looks up, to hide the evidence of the room swap: wet hair (towel), Vasco's shirt on Alan's back, the room 4 key in his pocket, the unslept bed in room 9 (send Inês up first). Each round adds one item; Sunday adds a very obvious love bite on Alan's neck if the player took the Darío night (scarf, collar, or Inês's concealer). Mireya always catches one, with a deadpan line, and feeds Alan more *mangú*. Monday she ends the game: "In my house the stairs are loud, señor. Eat."

### 9. Alan's titles (running gag)

The "who is this man" line escalates each act: *amigo de la familia* (Wed, Ramón) → "the decorative one" (Thu, Alan himself) → *el caballo* (Sun, Lilo) → *el portugués* (Mon, Ledesma). On the shore Fello introduces him to the Haitian side with all four.

### 10. Deductions (the notebook and the folder)

Alan's notebook holds what he has seen and heard as entries; Inês's document folder does the same for papers. Dragging one entry onto another tests a connection. A true connection writes a new deduction entry in Alan's hand (or a pencilled note in Inês's); a false one gets a dry line and nothing else. Deductions are how the game's cleverest puzzles are solved, so they never depend on a pixel hunt:

- **The overlay (ML3):** the pins + the two wet bands → "Every band ends at the tower."

- **The prediction (ML3):** that + rule 5 + "Tobías dries the bell every night" → "Tobías next."

- **The bearing (ML1):** the frieze sketch + "where the mark looks" → "Take a bearing from the arch."

- **New York (ML4):** the 1979 margin + "Fello's wife rings Mondays at six" → "Find Carmela."

- **The 1930 carbon (ML2, Inês):** "your request of the 28th ultimo" + the outgoing copybooks → "The cathedral asked."

Their shore (Sun): the victims Batista named + the notebook's names → "They were all on her shore." A second route to predicting Tobías, who is on the 1995 list. A deduction the player hasn't made is never required to be typed or guessed; the hint icon nudges toward the two entries that connect.

### Deaths and failure

The Sea can kill Alan in three places, Sanches-style: the market drain (ignore Yolande's cue three times), the gallera drum (keep the hand on the skin after Lilo says "*Quieto… ya, ya*"), and the bell-chamber on Sunday night (fail to throw the rum in time; Tobías crawls out, Alan doesn't). Each is a short, camp drowning cutscene: Alan's face, the water at his lips, a beat of black, Pessoa yowling in the empty shop in Lisbon. Then rewind to the moment before. Wrong actions elsewhere get a line and, where it fits, a comic cutscene (the parrot waking the house, Fello's "*He comes all the way from Europe for a cerveza!*"). The finale can fail by beat, never by game over.

## Multi-level puzzles

Six puzzles run across several layers, days, locations and both playable halves. Each layer leaves a visible partial result in Alan's notebook or the napkin, so the player is never holding nothing. Three (ML1, ML3, ML5) have fallbacks, so a missed layer slows the player down without blocking the game. Three (ML2, ML4, ML6) are on the critical path: they can be delayed, never missed.

### ML1 — The bearing (Thu → Tue; Alan and Inês)

*Question: where on the shore did Tata stand? Only Tata knew. The others met at her alley and followed her lantern down steps that Trujillo's Malecón buried in the 1930s; since April nobody can find the way (Yolande, Saturday: "She led. We followed the light.").*

1.  **The frieze (Thu tower, Alan).** The bell's frieze has twelve waves and seven little ships. One ship sits directly above the founders' mark, bow pointed off-true. Alan sketches it.

2.  **The second line (Thu archive, Inês).** Under raking light, the 1586 margin note has a second, fainter line: *a la boca del río, donde mira la marca* — "at the river mouth, where the mark looks."

3.  **The sighting (Sun sunset, Alan in the tower).** At the sea-side arch, Alan lines the ship's bow up with the mark and reads a bearing off Vasco's compass: 112°. Must be done before the bell comes down at dawn Monday; it can be done any evening Thursday to Sunday.

4.  **The plot (Tue, Inês at the hostal).** Inês draws 112° from the tower on the 1890 harbour map with Vasco's protractor and crosses it with the 1890 shoreline. The Guardia letter says only "*the shore at the river mouth*", which could be either bank; the bearing settles it. The line lands on the Playa de las Negras, now under Trujillo's Malecón and reached by forgotten steps.

*Fallback:* if layer 3 is missed, Tobías remembers his father taking the same sighting "*like a sailor*" and gives the bearing on Monday, at the cost of a sad line.

### ML2 — The 1930 trail (Sat; Inês)

*Question: who stopped the shore in 1930?*

1.  **The dead end.** The 1930 chapter acts hold nothing but money and Masses. Deliberate.

2.  **The date.** In the archive's own 1930 folder, a photograph of a radio-station plaque dates the San Zenón hurricane: 3 September.

3.  **The index quirk.** The *correspondence received* card index has no "Guardia Nacional" under G. A card seen earlier ("Sociedad Colombina" filed under C) teaches the quirk: Padre Ulises's predecessor filed by the *last* word. The card is under N.

4.  **The carbon.** The Guardia letter answers "*your request of the 28th ultimo*". Inês finds the cathedral's outgoing copybook for August 1930: the Archbishop's secretary's carbon, asking the Guard to "*disperse the superstitious gathering on the shore*". Vasco's "*Who asked them to?*" is answered on screen, with the pencilled *Gracias a Dios*.

Used in: the Paris dossier, Batista's evidence (M9), and the gate scene, where Moncho holds the carbon.

### ML3 — The pin map and the tower (Fri → Sun; Alan)

*Question: who drowns next?*

1.  **The map (Fri dinner).** Batista lists the five drownings; Alan marks them on the tourist map in his notebook.

2.  **The overlay.** Alan adds the two wet bands he has seen, the Calle Las Damas (Wed) and the Parque Colón (Fri). Every death is within a street of a wet band; every band ends at the tower foot.

3.  **The prediction.** Rule 5 says it strikes at those who understand the lock. Near the tower, who understands? Alan writes the answer in his notebook: Tobías, who sleeps by the cloister and dries the bell every night.

4.  **The rescue (Sun sunset).** Tobías goes up and doesn't come down. With the prediction made and a bottle of Barceló bought from Fello, Alan climbs. Tobías is on his knees under the bell, water pouring from her mouth to his chin. A Sea grip on *someone else*: this time the player acts, throwing the rum (rule 11 used, not watched). Tobías lives and comes to the hostal wet through: "*She's crying.*"

*Fallback:* without the prediction Tobías escapes alone, but he is too shaken to hold the tower door on Tuesday; Lalo takes it.

### ML4 — The call to New York (Sun → Mon → finale; Alan)

*Question: what will move Nando?*

1.  **The clue (Sun, Lilo reads it at the gallera).** The 1979 margin: *Wilfredo dio los nombres*. And: Carmela's family went to New York.

2.  **The refusal (Mon).** Nando shuts his door: "That's a Haitian thing."

3.  **The line.** Fello's wife cleans offices in New York and rings the colmado on Mondays at six (heard at the domino table Thursday). Alan asks Fello to ask her to find Carmela through her parish in Washington Heights.

4.  **The call (Mon 18:00).** A crackling line, passed from payphone to payphone. Carmela: Wilfredo died in 1990, and at the end he asked for Tata. Her message for her brother: "*Tell Nando he can stop being ashamed for both of us.*"

5.  **The delivery.** Nando won't open. Alan writes the message on a page of his notebook and Fello reads it aloud through the door, because Nando would never let a foreigner say it.

6.  **The crossing (finale phase 2).** Nando at the back of the Dominican side. Alan calls the message across the beach. Nando walks to Yolande.

*No fallback: this chain is required.*

### ML5 — The custody letter (Mon night → Tue dawn; Alan with Vasco and Inês)

*Question: how do you get a Haitian woman out of a police station at 5 am?*

1.  **The paper.** The Patriarchate crest paper from Lisbon. Wrong archdiocese; Vasco: "Nobody at a police station at five in the morning reads a crest."

2.  **The signature.** The bishop's welcome fax is still in Vasco's jacket. The power is out and dawn is an hour off, so Alan props a pane from the kitchen door in front of a lit St Michael candle and traces the signature over the glow.

3.  **The seal.** Inês kept the purple wax seal from the dinner invitation in her bag. Melt it with a St Michael candle and press it with the crest on the bishop's car-key fob.

4.  **The wording.** Vasco dictates in church Spanish; the player picks the three formal phrases, a callback to the Lisbon subjunctive puzzle.

5.  **The courier.** Tobías must carry it. The line that moves him is Tata's: "One day the people who made her will come back."

*Result:* Batista has gone home at four, so the letter must fool the Immigration desk sergeant before the 06:00 bus. A complete letter gets Yolande brought up from the cells; a letter missing pieces gets "wait outside" while the bus engine starts in the yard. Either way Batista walks in halfway through the bluff. He sees through any version, and Moncho's answer decides; with a complete letter he also puts Lilo's drum in the boot, otherwise the drums come back by the slower Tuesday chain.

### ML6 — Fello's drum (Mon; Alan)

*Question: how do you make a dead drum play by tonight?*

1.  **The skin.** The market tanner sells goatskin only to drummers, but his son Eddy, twenty-six, shirt open in the heat of the drying shed, sells to whoever makes him laugh. Charm (three right lines about drums, hands and leather) gets the spare skin thrown in; Lalo's note gets the first. Lalo will write a note only once Alan tells him Fello is coming back ("*Then I'm not the only one*").

2.  **The pegs.** Three tuning pegs are missing. Fello: "Guava wood. Only guava." The only guava tree is in Tata's yard, which opens after the blue-house scene (M5).

3.  **The tuning.** A new skin is slack. Alan holds Fello's St Michael candles under it to tighten it, tapping between heats and comparing the note to Lilo's drum. Three heats; too many and it splits (comic Fello despair, start again with the spare skin).

*Result:* Fello walks into the alley with his father's drum, "still pale and smelling of the tannery".

## The mystery

The first two games each had an impossible death, a list, layered secrets and a historical crime. This one has all four, and a twist of its own: nobody is killing anyone. The Sea is collecting, and the person holding it back is already dead.

### The central question

**Why are people drowning in dry places in Santo Domingo?** Two reasons. First, the Sea strikes first at whoever understands the lock: Tata, who kept it for sixty years, and then the others who had stood on her shore. Second, nobody paid this season, and the bishop is about to take the bell out of the tower, which would bring the whole 410-year debt due at once. Every victim is on one of Tata's lists in the notebook, which the player can discover as a deduction on Sunday.

### The red herring: Coronel Ledesma

For the middle of the week, the obvious suspect for the drownings is the man with the lists.

- **Fri:** he is at the watchman scene, talking quietly to the uniformed police, hands in pockets. Batista spits out his toothpick.

- **Sat:** Yolande names "the colonel of the list" from 1991. The notebook (Sun) says the guards came for the shore before, in the 1940s and in 1979.

- **Sun:** the notebook's 1979 page says the guards went house to house, and Friday's pin map overlay suddenly looks planned: all within a street of the cathedral.

- **Mon:** the raid. He is exactly what he seemed.

- **Payoff:** cruel, but innocent of this. Monday's tide that won't go out, and the bell pouring water in mid-air, clear him: no colonel does that. The bishop made the call; the Sea did the drowning.

### Series layer

Additions from *The Sea — Mythology of the Alan Sanches Series*. This bible wins wherever the two differ; these only add.

- **The Sea is never funny.** All comedy stays in the human layer, Mireya's parrot included.

- **The rule wording.** The series line is: *the Sea strikes first at whoever understands the lock.* Every character who states rule 5 uses those words.

## Prologue and Lisbon tutorial

### Prologue — Santo Domingo, 1586 (playable as Catalina Bran)

Ten minutes that teach the finale in miniature: the rhythm, the sign, and gathering people. Opens on the wrong bell ringing over the Ozama and the sea full of Drake's ships.

**Cold open (cutscene, 60 s).** Catalina and old Juana at the river. Tomé ringing and ringing. The English month in three painted stills: horses in the chapels, smoke, the drowned brother in his locked room with salt in his mouth.

**P1 — Watch the sea.** Catalina at the river mouth, three times across one summer (scene repaints). The player clicks the signs her mother taught her: frigate birds gone, dead fish in lines, the black patch beyond the reef, the cold smell. Each found sign adds a mark on a tally stick. Three signs open the path to Antón Congo.

**P2 — Antón Congo.** Dialogue. He lays out the rules: it comes in the season of storms, it can be turned not stopped, it needs drums, singing, as many people as you can find, and "someone to carry it". Catalina: "I am." *Rules 1, 2, 10 introduced.*

**P3 — Find the eleven.** A compact recruitment loop, the game's core in miniature. Six huts and kitchens; each person needs one thing:

- The Wolof boy: be told what he saw (the black water in the cane) is real. *Dialogue choice.*

- The Biafara woman: Catalina carries her wet cradle blanket to the river and back. *Use.*

- The two mill brothers: their drums are hidden from the overseer; find them under the cane trash. *Search.*

- The fish-seller: trade the canons' linen for her gourd. *Give.*

- Juana: already yes.

- Tomé arrives on his own ("Because I rang it"). Catalina: "Can you clap?"

Counter on the tally stick reaches eleven.

**P4 — The shore.** 29 September, storm. The Biafara woman draws the sign in ash on the rock. The player taps the rhythm with Antón Congo (first teaching of the three-and-a-rest, skip on the third). Halfway, the drums falter; the player must keep tapping alone for four bars while Antón shouts "*Don't stop, she's ours, sing her home.*" Then cutscene: Catalina walks into the sea; the darkness follows; dawn; she is breathing on the beach.

**Close (cutscene).** Years later, Catalina to grown Tomé: "*La campana es del mar.*" The bell in the dark tower. Title card.

**Design note.** The prologue ash sign is washed by rain at the end of P4 (Catalina has to redraw it once). This quietly seeds rule 9 for the finale.

### Tutorial — Lisbon, Thursday 29 August 1996 (Alan, then Inês)

Twenty minutes in reused game 1 assets. Teaches verbs, inventory, documents and the first switch. Everything here is a seed.

**L1 — Pessoa and the Eça.** Inês's last day. Pessoa has been sick on the second volume of *Os Maias*. Alan cleans it (kitchen paper from the flat) while Inês dictates. Teaches look/use.

**L2 — The index cards (collectible, 9).** Inês works shelf by shelf; each conversation topic drops an index card into Alan's inventory. Required: Porto dealer, Gulbenkian maps, electricity bill. Optional: the safe combination, the lonely Tuesday man, the dog-woman thief, the Belgian ("his wife is fine"), Pessoa and the Camões, "hire someone Pessoa tolerates". These are the finale's ammunition; the more collected, the more Inês can shout (minimum 3, all required ones are unmissable).

**L3 — The safe.** Combination = grandfather's birthday backwards. Alan doesn't know it (Inês: "You've rung me twice this year to ask"). Found on the back of a framed photo in the flat (game 1 asset callback). Inside: nothing needed, but a founders' mark on a deed matches the lintel.

**L4 — The electricity bill (optional, with payoff).** On the desk with the post. Alan can pay it at the Multibanco machine on the corner: key in the entity and reference numbers from the bill, then his PIN, which is on a Post-it Inês pretends not to have seen. Flag bill_paid. Payoff in the finale: "*Paguei-a*" if paid; if not, "*Vou pagá-la*" ("I'll pay it") and Inês hits him harder.

**L5 — The post.** Sort the bundle: bills, mother's alligator postcard (seeds St. Augustine), and *Patrimonio Sacro Iberoamericano* no. 31. Inês reads the item; the photo shows the mark. The player compares the photo's mark with the lintel over the shop door (look up from the street). Cut to Vasco's office.

**L6 — Switch to Inês: the ledger.** Inês's first playable scene. Vasco's office, the grey FOUNDERS box file. She finds the 1538 entry by matching the bell's weight (42 arrobas) and the destination to the ledger's index, telling two hands apart: Gaspar's and "his cousin, the one with the long s". Teaches document comparison. Result: "*One of the true ones.*"

**L7 — The letter.** Vasco drafts the letter to the bishop; Inês corrects his subjunctives (pick the right three of five forms). The player takes a sheet of **Patriarchate crest paper** from the stack (prompted by Vasco: "Don't waste those"). Item carried to Santo Domingo for the Tuesday bluff.

## Finale and epilogue

The finale is four phases on the Playa de las Negras, about 30 minutes, alternating Alan and Inês. Each phase cashes in something earned earlier. No phase can be lost; a failed beat replays with a stronger hint from Yolande, Lilo or Vasco.

### Phase 1 — The sign (Inês)

Yolande gives Inês the white chalk: "*Tú dibujas. La muchacha que lee.*" Inês starts on the rock; the rain takes it (rule 9, shown in the prologue and on Tata's step). Yolande: "*Dibújalo en él.*" Alan takes off his shirt.

- The player draws Agwe's boat on Alan's chest by placing the parts in order, using Alan's vèvè_sketch from Saturday as the reference shown at the corner. A good sketch means fewer parts to place; a poor one means Yolande corrects two of them.

- Inês: "You come back. I've just spent six years training you." — "Who'd look after Pessoa?" Forehead kiss.

- Vasco with Alan's shirt. Alan: "*Ainda não.*" Vasco: "Don't you dare." The kiss on the rocks. "So you'd better have an answer ready."

### Phase 2 — The two halves (Alan)

Alan wades in to his knees. The heartbeat is loud. Behind him, the beach is split: Dominicans singing the *salves* on the right, Haitians the Kreyòl on the left, not meeting. Moncho stands in the middle with the notebook, frozen.

- **Arrange the shore.** Alan can call out to people on the beach (cursor works over his shoulder). Required: Lilo playing *the one that calls*; Fello under him; the Haitian drummers on the left; Mireya leading the *salves*. Each placement steadies one drum or voice in the soundtrack.

- **Nando (earned).** He is at the back of the Dominican side. Alan calls *Carmela's message from New York across the beach (ML4)*. Nando walks across the beach to Yolande: "*Yo estaba en el setenta y nueve… Mi sobrino… Él le dio su nombre a los soldados.*" He holds out his hand; she takes it and turns to stand beside him. He sings the Spanish half beside her Kreyòl. **The halves meet.** Both sides cross the middle. 123 people on a beach the size of a room.

- **Moncho.** Still frozen. Alan's last action from the water: tap the rhythm on his own chest, over the drawn boat (the rhythm input). Moncho hears it, lifts his head, and sings his grandmother's song from memory. The Bran voice. The Sea stops beyond the reef, then comes in slowly — to Alan.

### Phase 3 — Batista on the steps (cutscene with one Inês choice)

23:00. Ledesma's vans, blue lights in the rain. Batista on the third step, a hand on the colonel's chest: "*Usted está retirado.*" — "*Mi mamá está en esa playa, Coronel.*" The young policeman who prised Lilo's fingers takes off his helmet and walks down to his grandmother; then another, and another. Ledesma leaves.

- If Batista committed on Monday this plays as above. If not, Inês must go up the steps and show him his mother on the beach (point her out among the crowd: the small woman from Gualey beside a Haitian woman). Then the same scene.

- **The fortress wall.** Only Tobías sees him: a tall figure in a black cassock, watching from the wall. The camera finds him for two seconds if the player looks up at the fortress; nobody comments.

### Phase 4 — Inside the Sea (Alan) and the list (Inês)

The longest phase. Control alternates every beat.

- **Alan (interior space).** The screen goes to a surreal sea-dark: cold, enormous, angry, 410 years old. Alan walks out over the reef with the darkness following. The Sea asks: *why should I?* Alan has no answer. The only action is to look back at the shore and see the people as it sees them: 123 tiny warm things holding hands. Something in it changes. Then it starts to go out, taking Alan.

- **The drums falter** (00:30). Lilo's hands slow; the beach wavers, as in 1586. Heartbeat skips.

- **Inês stands up.** Switch. She can't drum or sing; she can make lists. The player chooses index cards from the tutorial and shouts them in Portuguese, one per Alan beat. Each card shouted returns control to Alan for one step back toward the shore. Required three: the Porto dealer, the Gulbenkian maps, the electricity bill. Optional cards add a step each and comic lines inside the Sea (the Belgian: "*A mulher dele está ótima! EU CONHEÇO-A!*").

- **Vasco drums.** Alan's steps back are timed to Vasco's drum, three beats and a rest: the player taps to walk. Vasco never falters.

- **Moncho changes one word.** *Este para traerla de vuelta* becomes *traerlo*: bring *him* back. The whole beach takes it up. Alan laughs inside the Sea; the part of him that is Alan turns round. The Sea lets go "the way a hand opens".

- **The beach.** 01:10. A wave leaves Alan face down on the sand, not breathing. Batista's CPR; Yolande's second bottle ("*una pa' cuando venga el mar*"). Inês at his head, not shouting: "Alan. *A conta da luz.*" He coughs up the sea. Then bill_paid: "*Paguei-a.*" She bursts into tears and hits him. Not paid: "*Vou pagá-la.*" She hits him harder, then holds on.

### Dawn (cutscene)

02:00: Hortense bends north. 03:15: the bell rings once on her own in the empty tower; up the hill the vigil hears it as a miracle. Yolande: "*Ya está. Pagado. Por este año.*" Lilo smiling, alive. On the bottom step, under a police blanket: "*Is this Santo Domingo?*" — "*Agora. Pergunta-me agora.*" Alan still can't finish the sentence. Vasco: "Yes. Whatever the end of that sentence is." Inês, not lifting her head: "*Finally.*"

### Epilogue

**E1 — Santo Domingo, 11–14 September (montage, 90 s).** *Listín Diario*: EL MILAGRO DE LA VIGILIA; page fourteen cut out by Inês. The white boat won't wash off Alan's chest. Batista returns Yolande's card, new, and Lilo's evidence tag. Moncho dismissed in two lines; Fello gives him mornings at the colmado; Lalo pays the fees. The photograph goes back up face out. Moncho's terrible, perfect *dulce de coco*. The bishop unwell, receiving nobody. Playable beat: Alan may leave a card at the palace gate, written with Moncho ("She was my grandmother's friend too"), or leave nothing. If he leaves it, the 1997 candle on the rock has a second one beside it. From Ramón's taxi: a tall figure in the tower arch beside the bell.

**E1b — The last night (scene, fade to black).** 13 September, room 4, the hostal open again. The heat is back, the fan is back, the parrot is back next door. The white boat still hasn't washed off Alan's chest. Vasco traces it with one finger, hull, mast, sail, and asks if it hurts. It doesn't. Quieter than Wednesday, and a choice rather than a habit: this is the night the "Is this Santo Domingo?" question has been answered without either of them finishing the sentence. Mireya, through the wall: "Señores. The stairs are still loud." Fade.

**E2 — Lisbon, 22 October (playable, 3 min).** The Belgian and the Pombaline maps. The player chooses Alan's opener; the right one is the card: "*How is your wife?*" (in French). He tells the truth: Biarritz, golf. Real price, paid. Inês in the back-room doorway: "You're good at that." — "I learned from the best." The lonely Tuesday man applauds.

**E3 — Santo Domingo, 10 September 1997 (cutscene).** The birds leave the river mouth. Erika passes north. Two hundred and six on the shore. Moncho at the water's edge, a wooden boat round his neck; Yolande on his left; Fello on Lilo's chair with Lilo's drum (Lilo died in March in Lalo's hammock). Batista counting, his mother singing. Alan's letter on Tata's altar: *you've got it now. It's yours.* A tall figure on the fortress wall. At dawn Tobías finds a red St Michael candle on the flat rock.

**E4 — Lisbon, 12 September 1997 (playable phone call, game 4 hook).** 01:45, the shop phone. Alan's hand on his chest: three beats and a rest. Vasco and Inês on the stairs. "*Tell me about the bell.*" Title: *Alan Sanches IV*.

## Dialogue, tone and production rules

The novel is the tone reference from day one. This avoids game 2's problem of flat, rushed dialogue that needed a second pass.

### Dialogue rules

1.  **Scenes breathe.** Key conversations follow the novel's shape: arrival (what Alan or Inês sees and smells), a character beat, the information, the reaction, an exit line. Key scenes run 15–30 lines; minor ones 5–10.

2.  **Lift, don't paraphrase.** Where a scene has a matching passage in the novel, keep its best lines word for word, using the novel's English rendering and keeping one Spanish word where it fits. Spanish quotes in the day chains of this bible are reference wording; the script renders them in English under rule 6 ("Estaba mojada" → "She was wet, señor. All of her.").

3.  **Core and depth.** Each key conversation has a mandatory core plus optional "Ask about…" topics. Optional topics carry texture, never the only copy of a clue.

4.  **Follow-up questions.** If a player could ask "why?", someone on screen asks it.

5.  **Narrator on arrivals.** One or two lines of close-third narration the first time a location is entered, taken from the novel.

6.  **Languages.** All characters speak English. Dominican characters drop in a single Spanish word or short phrase in about 30% of their sentences (señor, mi amor, ay, Dios, mañana, cerveza, el obispo, la campana, ya está). Yolande does the same with Kreyòl (blan, Bondye, pitit) and Spanish. Alan, Inês and Vasco use the odd Portuguese word (meu Deus, ainda não, querido). The dropped-in word must be guessable from context or echoed in English; never two foreign phrases in one sentence, and never a whole foreign sentence in speech. Exceptions that stay in the original with an English subtitle: sung lines (the lullaby, the salves, the Kreyòl answer) and written text (the notebook, the 1586 note, letters, signs). Also kept in Portuguese, on purpose: Inês's list shouted into the sea, "A conta da luz" and "Paguei-a", and "Pergunta-me agora" at dawn.

7.  **The sulk is honest.** Alan's jealousy and Inês's cruelty are played straight; the comedy sits beside it, never instead of it.

8.  **Running motifs** must recur on schedule: "*You're good at that*" (Thu sting → Sun blessing → Oct payoff), "*Ainda não*", "*Is this Santo Domingo?*", "two bottles", "three beats and a rest", the parrot. Story facts, unlike motifs, are told once per source and each source adds something new: Tata's wet death only by Fello (gossip) and Batista (forensic), others refer to it; 1979 by Lilo (how it failed), Fello (the shame) and Nando (why); "the bell belongs to the sea" as set out under Rules of the Sea.

### Four more rules, carried over from game 2

1.  **Action beats, not just lines.** Write what people do between lines: Tobías touching his keys before he speaks, Batista moving the toothpick, Lilo drumming on his knee, Mireya looking over her glasses. Use \[pause\] for held beats. Silence is allowed. Nothing important is delivered in under five lines.

2.  **Every character sounds like themselves.** See the voice sheet. If a line could be said by anyone, rewrite it.

3.  **Alan's looks carry the fear.** His examine lines are funny on Wednesday and get shorter and stranger as the week goes on. Anything near water after Friday gets a line that isn't a joke. The joke he doesn't make is the strongest line in the game. Jokes about people near the water are allowed; jokes about the water are not.

4.  **The drownings are cutscenes.** Chago, Doña Luz and Pupo appear as short narrated cutscenes, ending on the victim's last line; the watchman is found, not watched. The Sea never speaks.

### Script format

The same format as game 2. **\[PLAY\]** marks where the player has control; **\[CUT\]** marks non-interactive cinematics; *Looks:* are the lines spoken when the player examines something; *Ask about:* lists optional topics. Voice line ids follow name_day_nn (alan_thu_01, tobias_thu_03, narr_thu_02). \[pause\] is a held beat with no speech. Spanish, Kreyòl and Portuguese words stay in the line as written; the subtitle shows them as they are.

### Example: Tobías on the river bench, Thursday (TH7)

The bar for every key scene.

**NARRATOR** (narr_thu_05): The bench faced the river and nothing else. Tobías sat at one end of it as if the other end belonged to someone.

**TOBÍAS** (tobias_thu_01): \[touches his keys\] You didn't say anything. In the shop. About the candle.

**ALAN** (alan_thu_09): It didn't seem like my business.

**TOBÍAS** (tobias_thu_02): Everything is everybody's business in San Antón, señor. \[pause\] That's why it was kind.

**TOBÍAS** (tobias_thu_03): On Wednesday, in the tower. Your hand on her. She went cold. She doesn't do that for the canons. \[pause\] You are one of the ones who made her.

**ALAN** (alan_thu_10): My family made bells in Lisbon. A long time ago.

**TOBÍAS** (tobias_thu_04): Then you know what I'm going to say. My father said it to me, and his father to him. Four hundred years of de la Cruz on the ropes. \[pause\] Never sold. Never broken. Never melted. Because she isn't ours.

**ALAN** (alan_thu_11): Whose is she?

**TOBÍAS** (tobias_thu_05): \[looks at the river\] Nobody ever told us that part.

*Ask about:* **St Michael** ("Every week. Rum, a cigar, a red candle. For sixty years she brought them. Since April, me.") · **The canons** ("They know. They don't look.") · **The coin** (only if Alan found it in W6: "From the old ones, señor. She threw them in the surf.")

**ALAN** (alan_thu_12): Who's "she"? Doña Tata?

**TOBÍAS** (tobias_thu_06): \[pause; the keys again\] She had a grandson. \[pause\] I've said too much on a Thursday.

**\[PLAY\]** If Alan asks about the grandson again: **TOBÍAS** (tobias_thu_07): "Ask the bell, señor. She'll tell you before I do." *(He stands and goes. He doesn't look back.)*

In Santo Domingo it lives in three places: Alan and Vasco, who are secure and play together; Alan's eye and Alan's skin; and the people who flirt with him. Innuendo, flirting and skin stay on screen. Intimate scenes fade to black for the App Store build; a web build can go further.

### Cultural and sensitivity rules

- **Ritual is practice, not power.** Dominican *vudú*, the *salves* and Haitian Vodou are things people *do* together. No spells, buffs, mana or "ritual items" with stats. The vèvè is drawn by characters in story moments only.

- **Agwe and the *misterios*** are named and respected; no lwa appears as a character or monster. The Sea is the series' own entity, not a lwa.

- **The 1937 massacre** is told by Yolande and Moncho and never shown or played.

- **Antihaitianismo and deportation** are depicted through Ledesma, the torn card and the bus; the game sides with the people on the beach without speeches.

- **Real politicians** (Leonel Fernández, Peña Gómez, Balaguer) appear only on posters, the radio and in jokes. No real cleric appears; the Cardinal-Archbishop of 1996 is never named (see continuity).

- **Consultant read.** A Dominican and a Haitian reader review the script and the vèvè art before lock.

### Decisions

1.  **Dialogue is English,** with Spanish, Kreyòl or Portuguese words dropped into about 30% of sentences; songs, writing and a few Portuguese moments stay in the original.

2.  **Two playable halves:** Alan (streets, people) and Inês (papers, institutions). The story chooses who plays; Vasco is never playable.

3.  **Native-speaker casting** for every accented role.

4.  **Darío appears in this game only,** and his night is optional.

5.  **The bishop is not punished on screen.** His ending stays ambiguous: the tower, the wall, the candle.

6.  **The Cardinal-Archbishop of 1996 is never named;** he is in Rome for the autumn.

7.  **The cockfight and the 1937 massacre are never shown.**

8.  **One ending,** with flag-driven variations in lines, never in outcome.

9.  **Lilo dies off screen in March 1997.**

10. **Game 4 is St. Augustine and family;** the epilogue phone call is the hook.
